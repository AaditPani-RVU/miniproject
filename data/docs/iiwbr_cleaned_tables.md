# Wheat Cultivation in India (ICAR-DWR/IIWBR Extension Bulletin 52, 2014): tables rewritten as sentences

Source: "Wheat Cultivation in India: Pocket Guide", Extension Bulletin 52, ICAR-Directorate of Wheat Research, Karnal (Kumar, Singh, Singh, Sharma et al., September 2014). This file restates the bulletin's tables as plain sentences so that each fact is self-contained. No numbers have been added or changed.

## Wheat growing zones and Uttar Pradesh

Uttar Pradesh falls in three wheat zones. Western Uttar Pradesh (except Jhansi division) is in the North Western Plains Zone (NWPZ). Eastern Uttar Pradesh is in the North Eastern Plains Zone (NEPZ). Jhansi division of Uttar Pradesh is in the Central Zone (CZ).

NWPZ covers Punjab, Haryana, Delhi, Rajasthan (except Kota and Udaipur divisions), Western UP (except Jhansi division), parts of J&K, parts of Himachal Pradesh (Una district and Paonta valley) and the Tarai region of Uttarakhand. Wheat area in NWPZ is 11.5 million hectares.

NEPZ covers Eastern UP, Bihar, Jharkhand, Odisha, West Bengal, Assam and the plains of North Eastern states. Wheat area in NEPZ is 11.9 million hectares.

Central Zone covers Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Kota and Udaipur divisions of Rajasthan, and Jhansi division of Uttar Pradesh. Wheat area is 5.0 million hectares.

## Wheat fertilizer dose in Uttar Pradesh

Fertilizer dose (khad, urea, DAP, NPK) for irrigated, timely sown wheat in western Uttar Pradesh (NWPZ): 150:60:40 kg N:P2O5:K2O per hectare, that is 150 kg nitrogen (N), 60 kg phosphorus (P2O5) and 40 kg potash (K2O). Apply one third of the nitrogen and all of the phosphorus and potash as basal dose at sowing, and the remaining nitrogen in two equal splits at the first irrigation and the second irrigation.

Fertilizer dose (khad, urea, DAP, NPK) for irrigated, late sown (after 25 November) wheat in western Uttar Pradesh (NWPZ): 120:60:40 kg N:P2O5:K2O per hectare, that is 120 kg nitrogen (N), 60 kg phosphorus (P2O5) and 40 kg potash (K2O). Apply one third of the nitrogen and all of the phosphorus and potash as basal dose at sowing, and the remaining nitrogen in two equal splits at the first irrigation and the second irrigation.

Fertilizer dose (khad, urea, DAP, NPK) for rainfed wheat in western Uttar Pradesh (NWPZ): 60:30:20 kg N:P2O5:K2O per hectare, that is 60 kg nitrogen (N), 30 kg phosphorus (P2O5) and 20 kg potash (K2O). All of it is applied at the time of sowing.

Fertilizer dose (khad, urea, DAP, NPK) for irrigated, timely sown wheat in eastern Uttar Pradesh (NEPZ): 150:60:40 kg N:P2O5:K2O per hectare, that is 150 kg nitrogen (N), 60 kg phosphorus (P2O5) and 40 kg potash (K2O). Apply one third of the nitrogen and all of the phosphorus and potash as basal dose at sowing, and the remaining nitrogen in two equal splits at the first irrigation and the second irrigation.

Fertilizer dose (khad, urea, DAP, NPK) for irrigated, late sown (after 25 November) wheat in eastern Uttar Pradesh (NEPZ): 120:60:40 kg N:P2O5:K2O per hectare, that is 120 kg nitrogen (N), 60 kg phosphorus (P2O5) and 40 kg potash (K2O). Apply one third of the nitrogen and all of the phosphorus and potash as basal dose at sowing, and the remaining nitrogen in two equal splits at the first irrigation and the second irrigation.

Fertilizer dose (khad, urea, DAP, NPK) for rainfed wheat in eastern Uttar Pradesh (NEPZ): 60:30:20 kg N:P2O5:K2O per hectare, that is 60 kg nitrogen (N), 30 kg phosphorus (P2O5) and 20 kg potash (K2O). All of it is applied at the time of sowing.

## Wheat sowing time and seed rate in Uttar Pradesh

Sowing time and seed rate for irrigated, timely sown wheat in western Uttar Pradesh (NWPZ): sow from 1 to 15 November. The seed rate is 100 kg of seed per hectare.

Sowing time and seed rate for irrigated, late sown (after 25 November) wheat in western Uttar Pradesh (NWPZ): sow after 25 November. The seed rate is 125 kg of seed per hectare.

Seed rate for rainfed wheat in western Uttar Pradesh (NWPZ): 125 kg of seed per hectare. The fertilizer for rainfed wheat is applied at the time of sowing.

Sowing time and seed rate for irrigated, timely sown wheat in eastern Uttar Pradesh (NEPZ): sow from 10 to 20 November. The seed rate is 100 kg of seed per hectare.

Sowing time and seed rate for irrigated, late sown (after 25 November) wheat in eastern Uttar Pradesh (NEPZ): sow after 25 November. The seed rate is 125 kg of seed per hectare.

Seed rate for rainfed wheat in eastern Uttar Pradesh (NEPZ): 125 kg of seed per hectare. The fertilizer for rainfed wheat is applied at the time of sowing.

## Wheat fertilizer dose and sowing in Central Zone (Jhansi division of Uttar Pradesh)

Central Zone (includes Jhansi division of UP), irrigated, timely sown wheat: sow from 10 to 20 November, seed rate 100 kg per hectare, fertilizer dose 120:60:40 kg N:P2O5:K2O per hectare. Irrigated, late sown wheat: sow from 1 to 15 December, seed rate 125 kg per hectare, fertilizer dose 90:60:40 kg per hectare. Rainfed wheat: seed rate 125 kg per hectare and fertilizer 60:30:20 kg per hectare, all at sowing.

## Wheat fertilizer, nitrogen split and balanced nutrition

How to decide how much fertilizer to use on wheat: first get the soil tested, in October or after harvest of the previous crop, so that fertilizer can be applied as per the soil requirement. Then follow the recommended dose for your zone and sowing condition: irrigated timely sown wheat in Uttar Pradesh needs 150:60:40 kg N:P2O5:K2O per hectare, irrigated late sown wheat needs 120:60:40, and rainfed wheat needs 60:30:20.

Do not apply more nitrogen than recommended. In the Karnal study, 71 of 100 farmers applied more than 150 kg nitrogen per hectare, which caused lodging and lowered yield and profit. Add zinc, manganese or sulphur fertilizer only where the soil is deficient in them.

Types of fertilizer for wheat (which fertilizers to use): wheat needs nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P2O5) and potash (K2O) in balanced proportion, along with farmyard manure (FYM). Nitrogen is commonly given as urea. Phosphorus is commonly given as DAP (di-ammonium phosphate); the Karnal farmers all used DAP, usually one 50 kg bag per acre as basal dose. Potash (K2O) is applied as basal dose.

Micronutrient fertilizers for wheat: use zinc sulphate for zinc deficiency, manganese sulphate (MnSO4) for manganese deficiency, and gypsum or Cosavet 80WDG for sulphur deficiency. Apply fertilizers based on a soil test and the recommended dose for your zone.

The recommended nitrogen:phosphorus:potash ratio is 4:2:1. Apply one third of nitrogen with all phosphorus and potash at the time of sowing, and the rest of the nitrogen after the first and second irrigation.

Zinc sulphate: apply 25 kg per hectare once a year, or 10 kg per hectare to each crop in the rabi and kharif seasons, in areas where zinc deficiency is common. If zinc deficiency symptoms are visible in the standing crop, spray 0.5 percent zinc sulphate solution: dissolve 2.5 kg zinc sulphate and 1.25 kg unslaked lime (or 12.5 kg urea) in 500 litres of water for one hectare and spray 2 to 3 times at 15-day intervals.

Manganese deficiency: spray 0.5 percent MnSO4 solution (2.5 kg MnSO4 in 500 litres of water per hectare). Give the first spray 2 to 5 days before the first irrigation and 2 to 3 more sprays at one-week intervals, on a clear sunny day.

Sulphur deficiency: more than 35 percent of soils are deficient in sulphur. Applying zinc sulphate or gypsum also corrects sulphur deficiency. Where only sulphur is deficient, apply Cosavet 80WDG at 8 to 10 kg per hectare at sowing or just before the first irrigation.

Seed and fertilizer should not be placed at the same place in the soil, because it hurts germination. If a seed-cum-fertilizer drill is used, fertilizer should be placed deeper than the seed.

Varieties marked with an asterisk in the bulletin are susceptible to stripe rust and should be avoided in stripe-rust-prone areas. Do not grow varieties from other zones, because of disease susceptibility risk.

Stripe rust (yellow rust) is the main disease problem in NWPZ. To avoid losses in NWPZ, prefer DBW 88, WH 1105, HD 2967, DPW 621-50, WH 542, PBW 550, PDW 314 (durum) and WHD 943 (durum) for timely sowing, and DBW 16, DBW 90, DBW 71, PBW 590, WH 1021 and HD 3059 for late sowing.

Old varieties that have become susceptible to yellow rust and should not be used: PBW 343, UP 2338, Lok 1, HD 2687, HD 2329, WH 711, HUW 234, UP 262 and WH 147.

Foliar blight is the main crop health problem in NEPZ (eastern UP). Recommended resistant varieties are HD 2985, HI 1563, DBW 39, CBW 38, NW 1014, NW 2036, K 9107, HD 2733, DBW 14, HD 2888, K 307 and HUW 468.

Ear cockle is important in eastern parts of India, especially eastern UP, Bihar and Jharkhand. Use gall-free seed. To separate galls, float the infested seed in 2 percent brine solution; galls float on the surface and should be removed and burnt away from the field. Wash the seed thoroughly to remove the salt before sowing.

Karnal bunt resistant varieties for NWPZ are PBW 502, PDW 233 and WH 896.

## Irrigation of wheat (Table 5)

Wheat normally needs four to six irrigations, which is 28 to 42 cm-ha of water. The two stages most sensitive to moisture stress are crown root initiation and flowering.

Irrigation schedule by stages: crown root initiation at 21 days after sowing (DAS), first node at 45 DAS, jointing at 65 DAS, boot at 85 DAS, milk stage at 105 DAS, and dough stage at 120 DAS.

Irrigation scheduling by water availability. With 1 irrigation: give it at crown root initiation (21 DAS). With 2 irrigations: crown root initiation (21 DAS) and boot stage (85 DAS). With 3 irrigations: crown root initiation (21 DAS), jointing (65 DAS) and milk stage (105 DAS). With 4 irrigations: crown root initiation (21 DAS), first node (45 DAS), jointing (65 DAS) and milk stage (105 DAS). With 5 irrigations: crown root initiation, first node, jointing, boot and milk stage. With 6 irrigations: all six stages, crown root initiation (21 DAS), first node (45 DAS), jointing (65 DAS), boot (85 DAS), milk stage (105 DAS) and dough stage (120 DAS).

Restricted irrigation, meaning a single irrigation after sowing under rainfed conditions, can double the grain yield of many wheat varieties.

In very light soil and in undulating land, use sprinkler irrigation. Bed planting saves about 30 percent water. Do not apply heavy irrigation to the standing crop, because waterlogging causes loss.

## Weed control in wheat (Table 4)

Apply herbicides about 30 to 35 days after sowing (DAS), except pendimethalin which is applied as pre-emergence at 1 to 3 DAS. Use a flat fan nozzle for uniform spray.

Grassy weeds (wild oat or Jangali Jai, Phalaris minor or Mandusi/Kanki, Polypogon, Poa annua): Clodinafop (Topik 15WP) 60 g active ingredient per hectare (400 g product), post-emergence at 30-35 DAS. Fenoxaprop-ethyl (Puma Super 10EC) 100-120 g a.i. per hectare (1000-1200 ml product), post-emergence. Pinoxaden (Axial 5EC) 40-50 g a.i. per hectare (800-1000 ml product), post-emergence. Sulfosulfuron (Leader 75WG) 25 g a.i. per hectare (33.3 g product), post-emergence. Isoproturon (Arelon 75WP) 1000 g a.i. per hectare (1333 g product), post-emergence. Pendimethalin (Stomp 30EC) 1000-1500 g a.i. per hectare (3333-4950 ml product), pre-emergence at 1-3 DAS.

Broad-leaved weeds (bathua, kharbathu, jangali palak, maina, senji, malva, makoi, hirankhuri, pyaji, chatri, matri, krishnaneel, pitpapra, kandai): 2,4-D ester (Weed Mar 38EC) 500 g a.i. per hectare (1315 ml product), post-emergence at 30-35 DAS. Metsulfuron (Algrip 20WP) 4 g a.i. per hectare (20 g product), post-emergence. Carfentrazone (Affinity 50WDG) 20 g a.i. per hectare (50 g product), post-emergence.

Both grassy and broad-leaved weeds: Sulfosulfuron (Leader 75WG) 25 g a.i. per hectare (33.3 g product), post-emergence. Pendimethalin 1000-1500 g a.i. per hectare, pre-emergence. Isoproturon plus 2,4-D ester, 750 + 500 g a.i. per hectare (1000 + 1315 g product), post-emergence. Sulfosulfuron + Metsulfuron (Total 80WG) 30 + 2 g a.i. per hectare (40 g product), post-emergence.

Where Phalaris minor has become resistant to isoproturon, use sulfosulfuron, clodinafop, fenoxaprop, pendimethalin or pinoxaden. Where Phalaris minor is resistant to isoproturon, clodinafop and sulfosulfuron, use pre-emergence pendimethalin.

Do not tank-mix grass herbicides (clodinafop, fenoxaprop, pinoxaden) with 2,4-D or metsulfuron, because of antagonism. Apply them one after the other instead. Do not under-dose herbicides, and do not use the same herbicide year after year in the same field.

## Disease and pest control in wheat

Stripe rust (yellow rust): spray propiconazole (Tilt 25EC at 0.1 percent), or tebuconazole (Folicur 250EC at 0.1 percent), or triadimefon (Bayleton 25WP at 0.1 percent) at the start of stripe rust, using 200 litres of water per hectare. This is usually needed in the first half of February. Early infection often starts under poplar trees in early-sown (October) crops, so watch those fields closely.

Loose smut and flag smut (seed treatment): treat seed with carboxin 75WP at 2.5 g per kg seed, or carbendazim 50WP at 2.5 g per kg seed, or tebuconazole 2DS at 1.25 g per kg seed. Alternatively use reduced carboxin (1.25 g per kg seed) with Trichoderma viride (4 g per kg seed). Treat with fungicide one or two days before sowing; apply T. viride 72 hours before sowing and fungicide 24 hours before sowing. Another option in the practical table is Raxil at 1.0 g per kg seed, or Vitavax at 2.0 g per kg seed, or Thiram plus Vitavax in 1:1 ratio.

Karnal bunt: one spray of propiconazole (Tilt 25EC at 0.1 percent) in 200 litres of water per hectare at 50 percent flowering; repeat after 15 days if conditions favour the disease. Avoid irrigation at heading time. Zero tillage reduces Karnal bunt.

Powdery mildew: one need-based spray of propiconazole (Tilt 25EC at 0.1 percent) at ear head emergence or when disease appears on the flag leaf, whichever is earlier.

Termite: treat seed with chlorpyriphos at 0.9 g a.i. per kg seed (4.5 ml product per kg), or thiamethoxam 70WS at 0.7 g a.i. per kg seed, or fipronil 5FS at 0.3 g a.i. per kg seed. In the standing crop, broadcast insecticide-treated soil about 15 days after sowing: mix 3 litres chlorpyriphos 20EC in 50 kg soil for one hectare. There must be enough soil moisture. Raised beds are more prone to termite attack; zero tillage shows less termite damage.

Aphids: spray imidacloprid 200SL at 20 g a.i. per hectare on border rows only, when aphids start colonising. In practical terms use 15 ml Confidor 200SL in 35 litres of water, applied 2 to 3 metres inside the field border. Do not spray the whole field, to protect the lady bird beetle that feeds on aphids.

Pink stem borer: more common in rice-wheat fields where wheat is sown by zero tillage. Spray quinalphos (Ecalux) 2000 ml per hectare as soon as the pest is seen. Irrigation also reduces damage.

## Resource conservation technologies

Zero tillage: sow wheat seed and fertilizer directly into undisturbed soil after rice harvest with a zero-till ferti-seed drill. It allows sowing 7 to 10 days earlier in late-sown conditions, saves more than Rs 3000 per hectare in cost of cultivation, and gives the same or 5 to 10 percent higher yield than conventional sowing, especially in sodic soils and late sowing. It also reduces Phalaris minor, Karnal bunt and termite. Seed rate and fertilizer doses are the same as conventional sowing. Avoid planking after sowing with this machine. The machine sows about two acres per hour.

Rotary tillage: prepares the field and places seed and fertilizer in a single pass. It saves more than Rs 2500 per hectare in field preparation, about 80 percent diesel, advances sowing by 3 to 5 days, and gives a 5 to 10 percent yield advantage over conventional and zero tillage if drilled. It covers about one acre per hour.

Bed planting: 2 to 3 rows of wheat on each bed; bed top width 40-45 cm, bed 70-75 cm with a 30-35 cm furrow. Sow in the North-South direction. It saves 20 to 25 percent seed (30 to 32 kg per acre is enough), saves about 25 percent nitrogen, and saves about 30 percent water. Avoid bed planting in heavy soils.

Laser land levelling: levels the field within plus or minus 2 cm. It saves 35 to 45 percent water, raises nutrient use efficiency by 15 to 25 percent, and increases cultivable area by 3 to 6 percent. The machine costs around Rs 3.5 lakh, so use it on custom hire.

Happy Seeder (Turbo Happy Seeder) and Rotary Disc Drill sow wheat into loose paddy straw without burning it. Turbo Happy Seeder handles up to 10 tonnes per hectare of residue, covers about one acre per hour, and needs a tractor of more than 50 HP with a dual clutch. It does not work when the residue is wet. Do not burn crop residue; burning kills beneficial insects and lowers soil fertility.

## Harvesting and storage

Harvest when the grain is hard when pressed between the teeth. The bulletin gives manual harvesting at grain moisture of about 20 to 25 percent, and says to use a combine harvester when grain moisture is below 14 percent. Harvest 4 to 5 days before the crop is dead ripe. Morning is the best time to harvest. Dry the grain to below 12 percent moisture before storage. Store in GI-sheet bins, aluminium bins, Pusa bins, silos or poly-lined bags. For storage pests, fumigate with aluminium phosphide at 3 g per tonne.

## Soil testing and general advice

Soil testing: do it in October or after harvest of the previous crop. Take samples from all corners and the middle of the field, mix well, draw a 500 g sample and get it tested at the nearest soil testing laboratory. Do not collect soil from compost heaps, residue heaps or standing crop.

Field preparation: start in October with good ploughing followed by planking, then level the field (laser levelling is beneficial). Do three to four operations and then level with a planker. A pre-sowing irrigation is given in October.

Timely sowing is very important; delayed sowing causes yield loss from heat near maturity. Choose a variety recommended for your area, buy healthy seed from reliable sources, and do not sow without seed treatment, otherwise smuts can cause heavy losses.
